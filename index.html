I am a theoretical physicist working at the interface of non-equilibrium statistical physics, active matter, and biophysics. I develop analytical and computational models to discover the fundamental principles governing living and driven systems: from bacterial motion and self-organizing cell aggregates to fluctuations, transport, and phase transitions in complex matter.

About

I completed my PhD at TIFR Hyderabad, India, where I studied active matter across different levels of description: single-particle dynamics, interacting many-body systems, and coarse-grained hydrodynamic theories.At the single-particle level, I obtained exact analytical results for active-particle dynamics and first-passage properties. At the many-body level, I studied how initial conditions, interactions, and activity affect transport and collective behavior. At the hydrodynamic level, I worked on fluctuation theory near active phase separation.

Then, during my Humboldt fellowship at HHU Düsseldorf, Germany, I extended these ideas toward memory effects and stochastic thermodynamics in active systems.

Now, as a Schmidt Science Fellow at MIT Cambridge, USA, I have been moving toward living matter, especially developing theoretical ideas for collective organization and self-assembly in marine organisms such as sea sponges.

So overall, my trajectory has taken me from rigorous theoretical modeling of nonequilibrium systems to biologically motivated and experimentally grounded problems in living matter.

Research

Self-organization in sea-sponge cell aggregates MIT · Now

Developing theoretical models of collective cell behavior in regenerating sponge aggregates, investigating self-organization and the emergence of multicellular structures in living systems.

Memory effects in active matter HHU Düsseldorf

Theoretical models of active particles with higher-order inertial and memory effects, with analytical results for transport, fluctuations, and emergent dynamics of chiral active systems.

Hydrodynamics & fluctuations in active particle motion Ph.D.

Theories of active matter across microscopic and macroscopic scales, exploring the interplay of activity, interactions, fluctuations, and initial conditions in driven systems.

Evolutionary models in population genetics

An exact fluctuating hydrodynamic theory for the stepping-stone model, a fundamental model of spatial population genetics.
